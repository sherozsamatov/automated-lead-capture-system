# 📋 Case Study: Automated Lead Capture System
**Client:** Clarity Coaching Co. (fictional client — portfolio project)
**Tools:** Google Forms · Google Sheets · Make.com · Gmail
**Time to build:** ~3 hours
**Ongoing cost:** $0/month

---

## 🔍 The Situation

Sarah Mitchell runs a solo business coaching practice helping freelancers and entrepreneurs grow their income. She's good at what she does — but her lead intake process was a mess.

Her website had a basic contact form. When someone filled it out, the submission landed in her inbox like any other email. From there, it was entirely on Sarah to:

- Copy the details somewhere she wouldn't lose them
- Reply manually to thank the person for reaching out
- Remember to follow up if she hadn't heard back

Some leads got a fast reply. Others waited days. A few fell through the cracks entirely. And at the end of the month, Sarah had no real picture of how many inquiries she'd received, where they came from, or how many she'd converted.

She didn't need a CRM. She needed a system.

---

## 🎯 The Goal

Build a lightweight, fully automated pipeline that:

1. Captures every inquiry the moment it's submitted
2. Logs it in a clean, trackable spreadsheet
3. Sends the lead an immediate, personalised response
4. Alerts Sarah with the full details so she can follow up when she's ready

All without Sarah touching anything.

---

## 🛠️ What I Built

`[screenshot: make-scenario-canvas.png]`
*The complete Make.com scenario — 4 modules, fully automated*

The solution is a 4-step automation built in Make.com that fires every time a new consultation request comes in.

---

### Step 1 — The Form

A Google Form simulates the "Book a Free Consultation" form on Sarah's website. It collects:

- Full name
- Email address
- Phone number *(optional)*
- What they're looking for help with

`[screenshot: form-preview.png]`
*The consultation request form as a visitor would see it*

---

### Step 2 — The Leads Tracker

Every submission is automatically written into a clean Google Sheet — the Leads Tracker. Unlike the raw form responses, this sheet is structured for Sarah to actually use.

Each row includes a timestamp, all the contact details, and two columns added by the automation: **Status** (`🟡 New Lead`) and **Source** (`Website Form`). As Sarah works through her leads, she can update the status manually — no other tools needed.

`[screenshot: leads-tracker.png]`
*The Leads Tracker after a handful of test submissions*

---

### Step 3 — The Thank You Email

Within minutes of submitting the form, the lead receives a personalised email from Sarah's address. It uses their name, echoes back what they wrote, and sets a clear expectation: *Sarah will be in touch within 24 hours.*

This does two things: it reassures the lead they haven't been ignored, and it makes Sarah look responsive even before she's looked at her inbox.

`[screenshot: thank-you-email.png]`
*The automated Thank You email received by the submitter*

---

### Step 4 — The Owner Notification

At the same time, Sarah gets an internal notification email. It contains the full lead details — name, email, phone, message, and timestamp — formatted in a clean table, with a direct link to the Leads Tracker.

Sarah opens one email, sees everything she needs, clicks the link if she wants to dig deeper. No hunting through form submissions or raw spreadsheet tabs.

`[screenshot: notification-email.png]`
*The notification email received by Sarah (the business owner)*

## 📊 The Result

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Leads captured manually (or not at all) | Every lead logged automatically with timestamp |
| Manual thank-you replies — hours later | Personalised response sent within 15 minutes |
| No central record of inquiries | Clean Leads Tracker Sarah can sort and filter |
| No visibility into volume or patterns | Status column makes pipeline visible at a glance |

**Time saved per lead: ~10–15 minutes** of copy-pasting, replying, and context-switching — multiplied by every inquiry Sarah receives.

## 💬 What This Would Mean for a Real Client

For a solo coach taking 10–20 inquiries per month, this automation saves roughly 2–3 hours of admin per month and eliminates the risk of leads going cold because of a delayed reply.

The setup cost is a one-time 3-hour build. The running cost is $0. And it works whether Sarah gets 2 inquiries a week or 20.

## 🔧 Technical Notes

> *Skip this section if you're not technical — the summary above covers everything that matters.*

- **Trigger:** Make.com watches the raw Google Sheets response tab for new rows, polling every 15 minutes on the free tier
- **Timestamp:** Generated by Make using `formatDate(now; "YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss")` — not pulled from the form
- **Status & Source columns:** Hardcoded strings added by Make, not user input — this is intentional so the data is consistent and filterable
- **Email sender:** Gmail module in Make.com — no SMTP configuration needed
- **Cost:** Runs entirely on free tiers (Make.com free · Google Workspace free · Gmail free)

**Known limitations in this build:**
- 15-minute polling delay (not real-time) — acceptable for a coaching business, not for high-volume e-commerce
- No duplicate submission guard — would add a filter module in a production build
- Gmail API limit of 500 emails/day — sufficient here, but Brevo or Postmark would be the upgrade path

## 🚀 Possible Next Steps for This Client

- **Lead scoring** — flag leads that mention specific keywords (e.g. "urgent", "budget", "ready to start") and route them to a priority list
- **Follow-up drip sequence** — trigger 3 automated follow-up emails at 24h, 72h, and 7 days if Sarah hasn't replied
- **CRM sync** — push new leads directly into HubSpot or Notion so Sarah has one place for everything
- **Booking integration** — replace the Thank You email link with a Calendly auto-booking so leads can schedule immediately
